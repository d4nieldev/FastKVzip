# Primary-source literature notes for the GraphKV draft

Research checked 8 September 2026. Sources below are papers, proceedings pages, or author repositories. The draft cites published venues when verified; otherwise it explicitly identifies the arXiv preprint. BibTeX has 28 core entries and two optional entries. `graphkv_related.tex` is approximately 600 words and can be shortened further for page balance.

## Material framing findings

- **The name GraphKV is already taken by a directly relevant paper:** Xuelin Li, Xiangqi Jin, and Linfeng Zhang, EMNLP 2025, [GraphKV: Breaking the Static Selection Paradigm with Graph-Based KV Cache Eviction](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1112/). It represents tokens as nodes, uses key similarity for edges, and propagates decay from selected tokens to reduce redundant selection. This is hand-designed graph-based selection, not the learned GNN plus two-stage selector training in this project. Avoid claiming the first graph-based KV eviction method. A separate [Graph-KV structural attention paper](https://github.com/Graph-COM/GraphKV) also uses a very similar name.
- **Two-stage reconstruction then response supervision has precedent in soft compression:** ICAE pretrains using autoencoding and language modeling, then instruction-fine-tunes. State the particular stages of this selector clearly without claiming that two-stage compression training is itself unprecedented.
- **Generation-supervised learned eviction has precedent:** DMS uses output-distribution distillation; TRIM-KV trains gates under a frozen backbone with predictive and distribution-matching objectives. Our distinction should be the combined graph selector, reconstruction warm-up, and answer adaptation for the intended cache-reuse setting, substantiated by ablations eventually.
- **RestoreKV is especially relevant recent work:** It learns restore tokens and adapters, with frozen base evictor, using full-cache answer distributions across sampled budgets. It adds synthetic KV entries; our work trains selection of original entries. Full-cache answer supervision alone is not a new contribution. Its objective is symmetric KL on answer distributions; ours is answer-token cross-entropy.
- **LCLM is valid:** Latent Context Language Models, introduced in *End-to-End Context Compression at Scale*, June 2026. This is a learned encoder--decoder soft-token compressor.
- **Head specialization needs calibrated language:** Voita et al. analyze encoder heads in machine translation, finding specialized important heads and redundancy. DuoAttention provides a more direct modern long-context-decoder example. Neither licenses saying that every layer/head has a unique function.
- **Fast KVzip's predictor is pointwise, but its inputs are contextual hidden states.** Do not say Fast KVzip ignores context or dependencies altogether. Our graph module adds explicit relational processing in the selector.
- **Gate equation and bias convention:** Section 3.1 gives the gate used by this model. The [Fast KVzip paper, Section 3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17668v2#S3.SS4), writes an additional nonnegative denominator term; the official implementation included in this project instead adds a learned bias to the token-key logit. The manuscript follows the latter parameterization, including the scaled dot products. The query projection has a bias, the key projection does not, and projected queries/keys receive learned RMS normalization. Reference keys are free parameters. Query responses are averaged within each KV-head group. These details were checked against `prefill/attention/gate.py` and its matching headwise adapter in `prefill/graph/model.py` on 8 September 2026.
- **BatchNorm convention:** The graph mixer uses featurewise BatchNorm over the tokens of each graph block, with learned affine parameters per layer/KV head, population variance, epsilon 1e-5, and current-block statistics during both training and inference. There are no running averages. This is separate from the gate's post-projection RMSNorm, whose scales are shared within a layer.
- **STE reference:** cite Bengio, Leonard, Courville (2013), arXiv:1308.3432, not the earlier Bengio-only arXiv:1305.2982 paper with a similar title. A straight-through gradient is a surrogate, generally biased; the actual hard top-k derivative is not made exact by the estimator.
- **Replay reference:** Chen et al. (2016) supports recomputation as a memory/computation tradeoff. Its particular asymptotic bounds cannot be transferred to this implementation without derivation. Describe our replay's own memory objects and bounds separately.
- **GIN reference:** cite its sum-aggregation/update design as inspiration. Weighted normalized low-rank graphs do not automatically inherit GIN's original injectivity or graph-isomorphism guarantees.
- **Agents motivate the problem but are not an experimental claim.** ReAct and MemGPT support accumulating observations/working memory. Avoid implying the present system has been evaluated on long-horizon agents absent such results.

## Benchmark guide added 9 September 2026

The statement above concerns the original literature prose. The experimental section now includes the completed result tables and a qualified, matched-task context-length comparison.

- RULER task definitions: [paper, including Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.06654v3) and [NVIDIA's concrete configurations](https://github.com/NVIDIA/RULER/blob/main/scripts/synthetic.yaml). The three single-needle and three multi-key variants are described separately; task meanings are shared across their length variants.
- SCBench task definitions: [SCBench paper, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.10319v2) and [official repository](https://github.com/microsoft/MInference/tree/main/scbench). The benchmark guide describes the [prepared KVzip dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Jang-Hyun/SCBench-preprocessed), not the original paper's unfiltered length statistics.
- Short tasks: [SQuAD paper](https://aclanthology.org/D16-1264/) and [GSM8K paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168). Split selection, context grouping, and the 72-token GSM filter are facts of the current local adapter, not properties of the official evaluation protocols.
- Length provenance: `.worktrees/ruler-evaluation/.slurm/grids/uniform-eval-all-w0/scbench-lengths.json` supplies complete raw-context Qwen measurements for all 27 SCBench variants. `dataset-inventory.json` supplies SQuAD/GSM measurements and all 78 RULER published-input distributions. A portable, full-precision extract with source hashes and revisions is saved in `data/benchmark-context-lengths.json`.
- Coverage, updated 10 September 2026, is the canonical 107-configuration inventory in the ruler worktree's `prefill/data/benchmarks.py`, compared with the 60 configurations in `data/method-scores.json`: 33 RULER and all 27 SCBench variants. The remaining 47 lack matched results (45 RULER, SQuAD, and GSM8K), rather than being proven never-run configurations. The 66 new SCBench evaluations and six upload jobs all completed successfully; all 1,080 score points match W&B and the original 38 configurations are unchanged. This paper update submits no new evaluations and makes no W&B writes.
- The simplified guide uses RULER's standard 4K/8K/etc. labels without the separate average-length table. SCBench averages are rounded to the nearest thousand and placed alongside task descriptions, with all 27 variants retained. Exact lengths and ranges remain in the archival JSON only; no measurements were discarded.
- The context-length paragraph recomputes the same seven tasks at all three RULER lengths from unrounded source scores. It does not claim statistical significance, architecture-only causation, or a monotonic advantage over every baseline.
